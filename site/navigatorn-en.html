Five European countries plan return hubs for rejected asylum seekers in Rwanda

17 November 2026 at 13:08 UTC. Published without manual review, machine source-checked.

Five European countries, Austria, Denmark, Germany, Greece and the Netherlands, plan to send rejected asylum seekers to a return hub in Rwanda. According to Premium Times, an initial pilot is expected to involve about 100 people, and human rights groups have criticised the plans.

How the world sees it 7 regions
The headlines highlight different aspects. African sources highlight Rwanda's role: AllAfrica, carrying text from New Times (Rwanda), writes about a partnership, and Premium Times (Nigeria) writes about a pilot project. European sources, including Deutsche Welle and The Guardian, mainly highlight the plan itself and criticism from human rights groups. The Japan Times (Japan) highlights protests from human rights groups and a warning from the UN refugee agency UNHCR. The National, a state-controlled outlet in the United Arab Emirates, headlines the plan as a scheme to be announced.

Sources differ: The sources describe the situation differently. Human Rights Watch writes about reported plans, Africanews about risks of violating international law, and The Guardian about plans to be announced. Daily Maverick (Reuters) writes that the plans were unveiled, while Al Jazeera writes about plans being revived. New Times (Rwanda, via AllAfrica) describes an announced partnership under which Rwanda may receive migrants while the parties explore return or settlement in Rwanda. Premium Times states that each country may send about 20 migrants.

Sources: The Straits Times, DR Nyheder, Africanews, Deutsche Welle, AllAfrica, and 9 more
