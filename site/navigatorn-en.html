Five EU countries plan return hub in Rwanda for rejected asylum seekers

18 November 2026 at 01:09 UTC. Published without manual review, machine source-checked.

According to several outlets, five EU countries, including Germany, Denmark, Austria, Greece and the Netherlands, plan a return hub in Rwanda for rejected asylum seekers. The UN human rights chief and Human Rights Watch express concern.

How the world sees it 3 regions
The sources highlight different aspects of the story. El Tiempo (Colombia) highlights reactions at the UN and in Europe, The Straits Times (Singapore) highlights the UN human rights chief, Deutsche Welle (Germany) highlights criticism from human rights groups, and The Japan Times (Japan) highlights protests and a warning from UNHCR. The East African (Kenya) and AllAfrica highlight the plan and the partnership itself, and Dagens Nyheter (Sweden) highlights that earlier attempts have failed.

Sources differ: The sources differ in how they describe the arrangement. The Washington Post says the countries have agreed to deport rejected asylum seekers to Rwanda, while The New York Times writes about plans. AllAfrica, citing New Times, reports that a partnership has been announced and that first transfers are targeted for 2027. Human Rights Watch and The Japan Times write about plans or reported plans.

Sources: The East African, DR Nyheder, Africanews, Deutsche Welle, The Guardian
