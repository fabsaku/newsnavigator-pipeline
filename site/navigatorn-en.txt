Spain train crash leaves at least 69 dead near Toledo
At least 69 people have died after a high-speed train hit the back of a stationary train near Toledo, Spain, on Wednesday, according to several outlets. Spain has begun three days of mourning, and search and rescue work in the wreckage continues.
How the world sees it: The headlines differ in what they highlight. Spanish sources (El País and El País América) highlight recordings of communication with the rail control center, where according to El País América the driver had warned that the train could not move, and criticism from the rail industry. British and French sources (BBC, The Guardian, France 24) highlight victims, grief, the search work and expert assessments of the causes. Chinese state-controlled media (CGTN) highlight, according to a first preliminary report, an estimated speed of 160 to 200 km/h at impact. Iranian state-controlled media (Tehran Times) highlight the death toll and state that, according to authorities, the crash is one of Europe's deadliest of this kind this century.
