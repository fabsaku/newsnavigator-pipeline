US to mediate talks between Russia and Ukraine in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11
US envoy Steve Witkoff says delegations from the US, Russia and Ukraine will meet in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11. According to him, the talks will cover post-war security arrangements, de-escalation and humanitarian efforts.
How the world sees it: The headlines differ in what they emphasize. Russian state media (RT) highlights that Trump's envoy announced new US-Russia-Ukraine talks and gives the dates. Emirati state media (The National) highlights Russia-Ukraine talks taking place this month, according to the US. Belgium's Euractiv links the talks to continued deadly Russian strikes. India's Hindustan Times highlights timing after the midterm elections, and Singapore's The Straits Times highlights that Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are expected to represent the US.
US: blue and red: Media that AllSides rates as leaning left (Axios) and right (Fox News) differ in wording. The Axios headline uses the word resume and describes the talks as a restart of peace negotiations, while the Fox News headline highlights that the US will mediate and that the talks concern security arrangements and de-escalation. The Axios lead explains that the earlier process was suspended in February due to the war in Iran and states that US officials see the meeting as a result of contacts between Trump's envoys and the parties. The Fox News lead cites no source beyond Steve Witkoff.

69 dead in Spanish train crash, twelve of them foreign nationals
According to Spanish authorities, as reported by The Guardian, 69 people died in the train crash on 28 October, 54 of them Spanish. Twelve foreign nationals are among the dead, four of them from the United States, according to several outlets.
How the world sees it: Norwegian NRK mainly highlights the number of foreign victims and which nationalities have been identified. Brazilian The Rio Times and Singaporean The Straits Times highlight the four American citizens among the victims. The Straits Times also notes that the crash was Spain's second high-speed train collision in 2026 and that it has raised new questions about rail safety. British The Guardian highlights that the crash is one of Europe's deadliest rail incidents this century, while BBC highlights that a scientist from the University of Cambridge is among the dead. Swedish SVT highlights that most of the dead have been identified.
