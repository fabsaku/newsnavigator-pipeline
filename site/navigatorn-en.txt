Canada says Russian and Belarusian players will not compete at world junior championship
Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney says Russian and Belarusian players will not be able to take part in the world junior championship in Canada. Several European countries, including Sweden and Finland, have reportedly said they will not play Russian teams.
How the world sees it: The headlines highlight different parts of the story. Kyiv Independent (Ukraine) highlights that Canada will use immigration law to keep Russian and Belarusian athletes from competing. The Guardian (UK) highlights that Canada has joined a coalition of countries refusing to play Russian teams. Hindustan Times (India) highlights the prime minister's announcement and several countries threatening a boycott. RT, a state-controlled Russian media outlet, highlights that four Nordic countries refuse to play Russia and that Canada opposes Russia's return.

Russia bombards Ukraine's power grid ahead of Abu Dhabi peace talks
Russia struck Ukraine's energy infrastructure with missiles and drones, according to Reuters, a week before US-brokered talks. Witkoff says the US, Russia and Ukraine will hold talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November.
How the world sees it: The headlines highlight different parts of the news. Russian state media (RT) highlights Witkoff announcing the next round of negotiations. Emirati state media (The National) highlights the US saying the Abu Dhabi talks will take place this month. Chinese state media (CGTN) highlights the US bringing together delegations from Ukraine and Russia to discuss ending the conflict, post-conflict security agreements and de-escalation. The Kyiv Independent highlights the European Commission hosting talks on Ukraine in Brussels on 4 November, and Euractiv highlights talks between the US, Europe and NATO in Brussels. The Japan Times and Al-Monitor highlight Russia's attacks on Ukraine's power grid and Merz urging Moscow to engage.
US: blue and red: Media that AllSides rates as leaning left (Axios) and as right (Fox News) highlight different things.
