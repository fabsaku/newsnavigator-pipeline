Vance says Iran war was reason Republicans lost midterms

5 November 2026 at 23:09 UTC. Published without manual review, machine source-checked.

US Vice President JD Vance says the Iran war was the real reason Republicans lost the midterm elections, according to several outlets. According to Middle East Eye, Israel's UN ambassador rejects his analysis.

How the world sees it 7 regions
The British BBC highlights that Vance's criticism is one of the sharpest breaks with a sitting president in recent memory. German Deutsche Welle highlights Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, who according to DW faces pressure from Republicans and is backed by Trump 'for now'. Qatari Al Jazeera highlights Vance's path toward 2028 and Republicans considering a change of course. Indian Hindustan Times highlights that Iranian officials use the defeat to argue the war has cost the United States politically and economically. Iranian state media (Tehran Times) highlights Vance's statement as support for the claim that the war explains the loss. The UAE outlet The National, which is state-controlled, highlights oil prices rebounding while uncertainty over the war remains. Spanish El País highlights that Republican Tom Massie plans an impeachment process against Hegseth, according to the paper.

Sources differ: According to Middle East Eye, Israel's UN ambassador rejects Vance's claim that the war was the real reason for the Republican midterm losses.

Sources: El País América, Middle East Eye, Deutsche Welle, Al Jazeera, Hindustan Times, and 8 more

Iran says ships caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz

6 November 2026 at 01:10 UTC. Published without manual review, machine source-checked.

Iran says five oil tankers and a tugboat caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, according to statements from the Revolutionary Guards and the army. Al-Monitor, citing Reuters, reports that three tankers were damaged.

How the world sees it 3 regions
Al Jazeera (Qatar) highlights Iran's claims and fears of a new US attack. The Hindu (India) also relays Iran's claims, citing five tankers and a tugboat. Sveriges Radio Ekot (Sweden) reports burning vessels without naming a source. Al-Monitor (US) and The Straits Times (Singapore) highlight continued pressure on shipping and the political cost of the US midterm vote, respectively. SCMP (Hong Kong) names the Revolutionary Guards and the army as sources and mentions naval mines. The National (UAE, state-controlled media) highlights Iran's claimed explosions as talks intensify.

Sources differ: The sources differ on the number of vessels. Al-Monitor (Reuters) reports three damaged tankers, while SCMP and The Hindu relay Iran's claim of five tankers and a tugboat. SCMP writes that the tankers struck naval mines, while the other sources do not state a cause. The National reports a series of explosions that Iran claims.

Sources: The Hindu, Middle East Eye, Deutsche Welle, The Straits Times, Al-Monitor

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña resigns after bank collapse

6 November 2026 at 07:09 UTC.

Paraguay's President Santiago Peña has resigned after the collapse of Ueno Bank, according to several sources, which report that millions of customer accounts have been frozen. According to NPR and the BBC, lawmakers have voted to pursue impeachment proceedings against him.

How the world sees it 6 regions
The headlines highlight different aspects of the event. Ekot (Sweden) focuses on the impeachment, while SVT (Sweden) and Deutsche Welle (Germany) highlight the resignation and the bank crisis. The Japan Times (Japan), the Buenos Aires Times (Argentina) and RT (Russia, state-controlled media) describe Peña as an ally of Donald Trump. RT also reports that the fall of the bank triggered mass protests, which none of the other sources reviewed mention in their ingress.

Sources differ: The sources differ on timing and on how the impeachment is described. NPR and France 24 report that Peña was impeached minutes and moments, respectively, after his resignation, while the South China Morning Post writes that he stepped down hours before lawmakers moved ahead with their process. Ekot's headline says the president faces impeachment, and the Rio Times writes that he said he will quit. Hindustan Times reports that the attorney general ordered a criminal investigation, while its headline refers to possible criminal charges.

Sources: Deutsche Welle, Al Jazeera, The Guardian, Hindustan Times, The Hindu, and 9 more

Tech stocks fall on fears of AI rules after US midterms

6 November 2026 at 12:16 UTC.

Tech stocks fell on Thursday after US midterm results sparked fears of tighter AI rules. Daily Maverick, citing Reuters, says the drop is the biggest since last year's sell-off.

How the world sees it 3 regions
The headlines highlight different parts of the event. The Guardian presents it as part of its morning news, focusing on regulatory concerns. Daily Maverick, citing Reuters, highlights the comparison with last year's sell-off and a months-long slump in AI stocks this autumn. Hindustan Times lists specific indices (Nasdaq, Dow, S&P 500) and sectors (chipmakers, AI data centre companies) that fell. Al Jazeera, France 24 and Breakit focus on the decline itself and link it to US rules or the election.

Sources differ: The sources agree that stocks fell and that the concern relates to AI rules, but they describe the scale differently. The Guardian uses the word "wobble", while Al Jazeera, France 24 and Breakit use wording that indicates a sharp fall ("plunge", "rasar"). Daily Maverick says the decline is the biggest in a year, while Hindustan Times does not make a comparison with earlier periods.

Sources: Dawn, The Guardian, Al Jazeera, Yonhap, Nikkei Asia (paywall)

Nasdaq falls sharply after US midterms on AI concerns

6 November 2026 at 01:10 UTC. Published without manual review, machine source-checked.

The Nasdaq fell sharply after the US midterm elections, by 5.5 percent according to SCMP and Nikkei Asia. According to BBC, the concern is possible tougher scrutiny of data centres and the AI industry.

How the world sees it 3 regions
The British BBC highlights possible tougher scrutiny of data centres and the AI industry after the election. South Korean Yonhap highlights that Seoul stocks opened sharply lower. Hong Kong-based SCMP highlights the size of the drop in dollar terms and the comparison with the deepest one-day fall since April 2025. Japan's Nikkei Asia highlights fears of AI regulation. The UAE's The National, a state-controlled medium, highlights investors selling AI stocks after the election.

Sources differ: The sources give different figures for the Nasdaq decline: 5.5 percent according to SCMP and Nikkei Asia, 5 percent according to The National.

Sources: BBC World, Yonhap, South China Morning Post, Nikkei Asia (paywall), The National (state-controlled)
