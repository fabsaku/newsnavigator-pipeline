Trump offers UFC chief Dana White White House press secretary job: White's response unclear

8 November 2026 at 02:08 UTC. Published without manual review, machine source-checked.

Donald Trump says he has offered UFC CEO Dana White the job of White House press secretary and urges criminal probes of Democrats, according to several outlets. The White House has had no press secretary since October 16.

How the world sees it 6 regions
The sources highlight different parts. US outlets highlight the offer and Trump's own post. Japan Times (Japan) and CNA (Singapore) highlight Trump's call for criminal probes of Democrats. Dawn (Pakistan) and The Straits Times (Singapore) highlight Trump's request that White bring some fighters along as assistants. South China Morning Post (Hong Kong) highlights Trump's combative tone ahead of an incoming Democratic Congress. Hindustan Times (India) and NRK (Norway) highlight that White declined. State-controlled Russian RT highlights the offer and White's longtime friendship with Trump.

Outlets that AllSides rates as right: New York Post (right) has the headline that Dana White says he is flying to Washington right after the offer. Washington Examiner (leans right) quotes Trump's post, which calls White a violent fighter and warrior, and describes him as a longtime ally. Outlets that AllSides rates as leaning left: The New York Times (leans left) has the headline that Trump asks Dana White of the UFC to be his press secretary, and The Washington Post (leans left) has the headline that Trump says he offered the White House press secretary role to Dana White. Outlets that AllSides rates as center: CNBC (center) and The Hill (center) describe the offer, The Hill in a live update that also covers other events that evening. The Wall Street Journal (center) focuses on White planning to meet Trump on Monday.

Sources differ: Outlets differ on whether White has responded. Hindustan Times and NRK report that he declined, and El País, citing CNN, says the same. Deutsche Welle writes that he has neither accepted nor declined. The Wall Street Journal reports that White plans to meet Trump on Monday, and the New York Post reports that he is flying to Washington. El País, citing TMZ, says the two will meet next week.

Sources: France 24, Hindustan Times, Rappler, NPR, The Hindu, and 19 more

New bomb threats hit flights and broadcasters across Europe

7 November 2026 at 11:15 UTC.

Several flights in Europe have been disrupted by bomb threats over the past week, according to several outlets. Broadcasters in several countries also received threats, and no bombs were found at the affected sites, SCMP reports.

How the world sees it 4 regions
US sources such as CNBC and NPR highlight in their headlines that the threats are difficult to contain and that they reach flights and broadcasters in Europe, while The Daily Caller highlights the number of diverted flights and evacuated newsrooms. Bangkok Post links the threats to a 20 to 30 percent market contraction caused by rising travel costs. Chinese state media (CGTN) asks in its headline what lies behind the wave and writes that security analysts suggest false warnings could create uncertainty among passengers.

Sources differ: According to The Hindu, the Irish government considers the threats likely to be a coordinated campaign. According to Meduza, Vladislav Pozdnyakov, founder of the Men's State movement, denies involvement in the bomb threats. RÚV reports that the bomb threat against Keflavík Airport is not thought to be linked to threats abroad.

Sources: Hindustan Times, The Guardian, Focus Taiwan, Sveriges Radio Ekot, Fox News
