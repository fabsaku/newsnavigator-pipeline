Polish president calls crisis meeting after clash at court

Polish President Karol Nawrocki calls a crisis meeting after unrest at the constitutional court, according to Swedish Radio's Ekot. According to Ekot, the government tried on Friday to remove the court's head, and thousands of demonstrators gathered outside the building.

How the world sees it 4 regions
Russian state-controlled media (RT) highlight that the president entered a besieged court and that the Constitutional Tribunal accuses Donald Tusk of a coup. Indian outlet Hindustan Times highlights a video of the confrontation and describes Anna Korwin-Piotrowska as a Tusk-backed court head. Polish outlet Notes from Poland highlights the physical confrontation with judges. Swedish Radio's Ekot highlights the government's attempt to remove the court's head and the thousands of demonstrators outside the building.

Sources differ: The sources describe the events at the court differently. Notes from Poland writes of a physical confrontation between the president and judges. Hindustan Times writes that video shows the president confronting Anna Korwin-Piotrowska, whom it calls the court's head, and that police surrounded the court. RT writes that the president stormed into a besieged court. The Straits Times and Euractiv do not describe what happened at the court, only that the president cut short a trip to Senegal and returned to Warsaw. RT also writes that the Constitutional Tribunal has accused Prime Minister Donald Tusk of a coup. Dagens Nyheter has published only a headline, which refers to accusations of a coup without naming who makes them.

Sources: Al Jazeera, The Hindu, The Straits Times, Notes from Poland, Euractiv, and 5 more

Iranian envoys meet Qatari mediators in Doha

Published without manual review, source-checked.

Iranian negotiators held talks with Qatari mediators in Doha, according to a source briefed on the talks (Reuters via Al-Monitor). The Straits Times reports that US President Donald Trump has held off on striking Iran ahead of the November 3 polls.

How the world sees it 4 regions
Al-Monitor (USA) and The Straits Times (Singapore) headline Iran's talks with Qatar and the threat of US strikes. Le Monde (France) instead headlines the US midterm elections, saying momentum shifts to the Democrats in Arizona. Sveriges Radio (Sweden) has a headline about its own coverage of the US midterm elections.

Sources: Sveriges Radio Ekot, Al-Monitor, The Straits Times, Le Monde (English) (paywall)

Flávio Bolsonaro says from the US that he will run for president again

Flávio Bolsonaro is in the United States and says, according to El País, that he will again be a presidential candidate. According to El Tiempo, he left Brazil after a court order requiring him to hand over his passport, amid an investigation into alleged money laundering.

How the world sees it 3 regions
The Spanish outlet El País highlights Flávio Bolsonaro's statement about his candidacy and his own account of political persecution. The Colombian outlet El Tiempo highlights that he left Brazil after a court order and the money-laundering investigation described in connection with it. The Indian outlet Hindustan Times highlights in its headline his route to the US via Argentina and the diplomatic measures between Brazil and Argentina.

Sources differ: Hindustan Times reports that Bolsonaro travelled to the US via Argentina, that Brazil summoned Argentina's representative, and that Argentina recalled its ambassador. Only Hindustan Times describes these diplomatic steps. Its headline refers to Bolsonaro without a first name, while El País and El Tiempo refer to Flávio Bolsonaro.

Sources: Hindustan Times, Yonhap, Middle East Eye, RT (state-controlled), The National (state-controlled)

Independent Russian newspaper Novaya Gazeta shuts down

The independent Russian newspaper Novaya Gazeta is closing, its editors say. The Straits Times and The Hindu attribute the closure to state and Kremlin pressure, and Meduza reports that journalists said the paper was to be evicted from its Moscow office the day before.

How the world sees it 4 regions
Singapore's The Straits Times and India's The Hindu headline that the paper is closing under pressure, citing state pressure and Kremlin pressure respectively. The UK's BBC highlights the founder's statement that authorities have stifled the paper. Meduza (Latvia) highlights whether the paper can rise again and the editor's view of what comes next. Sweden's SVT only reports that the editorial team is announcing the closure.

Sources differ: The sources do not contradict each other about the closure. They describe the cause differently: The Straits Times cites state pressure, The Hindu cites Kremlin pressure, and BBC highlights the founder's statement that authorities strangled the paper. SVT gives no cause.

Sources: The Hindu, Hindustan Times, Meduza, The Straits Times, The Kyiv Independent, and 4 more

One dead and six injured in shooting at Spirit Halloween store in Fairfield, California

Police said one person was killed and six injured when a gunman opened fire at a Spirit Halloween store in a mall in Fairfield, California, on Friday evening. According to Dawn, investigators had not yet identified the person responsible.

How the world sees it 5 regions
The US source New York Post highlights that the FBI has been deployed. Colombian El Tiempo highlights that the suspect is at large and that three of the injured are in critical condition. Pakistani Dawn highlights police statements and that the shooting occurred shortly before 8:30pm local time. Singaporean CNA highlights that the shooting took place hours before Halloween, while The Straits Times highlights that at least three people are in critical condition. Indian Hindustan Times highlights that at least seven people were shot and that the victims were found in three locations.

Sources differ: Dawn reports that investigators had not yet identified the person responsible. El Tiempo reports that the suspect is at large. Hindustan Times states that at least seven people were shot and that the victims were found in three locations, which matches one dead and six injured.

Sources: ABC News (Australien), El País América, NPR, The Rio Times, El Tiempo, and 3 more

Actor Patrick Stewart dies at 86

Published without manual review, source-checked.

Actor Patrick Stewart has died aged 86, according to several outlets, including Reuters and Deutsche Welle. He was known for playing Captain Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek: The Next Generation and Professor Charles Xavier in the X-Men films.

How the world sees it 6 regions
Sources from different countries highlight different parts of Stewart's career. Ars Technica (USA) and RNZ (New Zealand) publish personal tributes about his significance. The Hindu (India) and Daily Maverick (South Africa, with Reuters text) describe him as a Shakespearean stage actor who became a star of Star Trek and X-Men. Deutsche Welle (Germany) and Expressen (Sweden) highlight mainly Star Trek. NRK (Norway) mentions Star Trek and X-Men. El País América (Spain) highlights his career in pictures. Aftonbladet (Sweden) highlights tributes from fellow actors. The National (United Arab Emirates, state-controlled media) highlights Star Trek and X-Men.

Sources: Ars Technica, The Hindu, El País América, Aftonbladet Nöjesbladet, Daily Maverick, and 4 more
